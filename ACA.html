How to read this map

This map shows air quality right now across the Alberta Capital Airshed.

The colours

Every number is the AQHI, the Air Quality Health Index: a score for health risk from air pollution.

1–3Low. A great day to be outside. 4–6Moderate. Most people are fine. If you have heart or lung problems, take it easier outside. 7–10High. Cut back on hard exercise outside, especially if you feel unwell. 10+Very high. Put off hard activity outside. People at risk should avoid it.

What you see on the map

2Big circles are official monitoring stations. The number is the AQHI right now.
Small circles are low-cost PurpleAir sensors. The number is an estimated AQHI (see below).
Shading is our best estimate of the AQHI in the areas between monitors.
Grey means no reading right now.

How the sensors estimate the AQHIThe small circles

PurpleAir sensors measure fine particles, like smoke and dust. But the AQHI also depends on two gases, ozone and nitrogen dioxide, which these sensors can’t measure.

So we combine each sensor’s reading with the ozone and nitrogen dioxide levels in the area, from Environment and Climate Change Canada and nearby stations. Together they give an estimated AQHI for that spot.

We also check each sensor’s readings and leave out ones that look faulty.

It is an estimate, not an official reading.

How the shading fills the gapsThe AQHI grids

Monitors are far apart, so we fill in the map between them. Each small square takes its value from the monitors around it. Closer monitors count more.

  • “Stations” uses official stations only.
  • “Stations + Sensors” also uses the PurpleAir estimates, each counting half as much as a station. This one is on by default.

Pick an area

Grids are ready for Alberta, the ACA, Edmonton, Parkland County or Strathcona. Smaller areas use smaller squares, for more detail.

Good to know

  • Hover over a square to see its AQHI.
  • A dashed outline means one station reads very differently from its neighbours. It could be a real local event or an instrument problem.
  • The grids update every hour.
eAQHI (PurpleAir)Filling in stations that can’t give a full AQHI

A full AQHI needs three things: ozone, nitrogen dioxide and fine particles. Some stations are missing one. This layer fills them in.

They show as big circles with a dashed edge.

  • No fine particle monitor at the station: we use the station’s own gases, plus fine particles from up to 4 PurpleAir sensors within 20 km. Closer sensors count more, and odd readings are dropped.
  • Missing from the government feed: we use the airshed’s own backup data feed instead.
  • Station fully offline: we estimate from nearby sensors only.

Radar, Winds, Lightning and Thunderstorm Outlook

From Environment and Climate Change Canada. Radar shows rain and snow. Winds shows wind speed. Lightning shows recent strikes. Thunderstorm Outlook shows where storms are expected.

ECCC Alerts

Weather alerts from Environment and Climate Change Canada.

  • Yellow: advisory or statement
  • Orange: watch
  • Red: warning

Hover over an alert to see its name, the area, and when it starts and ends.

FireSmoke (March to October)

A wildfire smoke forecast from FireSmoke Canada. Darker colours mean more smoke. It is only shown in smoke season.

Heads-up banner

If there are active alerts or fires nearby, a banner appears at the top. Click its link to show them.

Where does this data come from?

Official stations: Alberta’s air monitoring network. Sensors: PurpleAir. Regional ozone and nitrogen dioxide, weather and alerts: Environment and Climate Change Canada. Smoke forecast: FireSmoke Canada. Readings are near real time and have not been through final quality checks, so they can change later. For the official AQHI, check the nearest station.
